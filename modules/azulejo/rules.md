# Azulejo

Draft painted tiles and fit them into a patterned wall. Two to five players. The game ends when somebody completes a row of the wall, and the highest score wins.

## Starting

- One player creates a game and shares the three-word code (or the invite link). The host chooses **how many seats** there are (2 to 5), fills them with friends or **computer players**, and starts the game once every seat is taken.
- The bag holds **100 tiles**: 20 each of five colours. Every colour also has its own pattern, so no tile depends on colour alone.
- There are **2 x players + 1** display plates (5, 7, 9 or 11). At the start of every round each plate is filled with 4 tiles drawn blindly from the bag. When the bag is empty, the lid (every tile discarded so far) is shuffled back into it. If both are empty, play on with what there is.
- Everything on the table is public except the contents and order of the bag.

## Your board

- **Pattern lines**: five lines holding 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 tiles. A line holds only one colour at a time.
- **Wall**: five rows of five places. Each place has a faint ghost colour showing which tile belongs there; every row is the row above, shifted one place along.
- **Floor line**: seven slots costing -1, -1, -2, -2, -2, -3 and -3 points. Tiles that fall on the floor are penalties.

## A round

- Play goes clockwise, starting with whoever holds the **first-player marker** (the first round's starter is random).
- On your turn take **all the tiles of one colour** from **one plate**. The other tiles on that plate slide into the **centre**. Or take **all the tiles of one colour from the centre**.
- The first player to take from the centre each round also takes the **first-player marker**. It goes on their floor line (taking the next free slot), and they start the next round.
- Then place the tiles you took on **one pattern line**. It must be empty or already hold that colour, must not be full, and the wall row for that line must not already hold that colour.
- Tiles that do not fit go to your floor line. You may also put all of them on the floor, and you must if no line is legal. Floor tiles beyond the seventh slot go to the lid without further penalty.
- The round ends when every plate and the centre are empty.

## Wall-tiling

- Working from the top line to the bottom, each **full** pattern line moves its **rightmost tile** onto the wall in its row, in the place for its colour. The other tiles of that line go to the lid. Lines that are not full keep their tiles for the next round.
- Each tile scores at once. A tile with no neighbour above, below or beside it scores **1**. Otherwise it scores the length of its horizontal run (counting itself) if that is more than 1, **plus** the length of its vertical run if that is more than 1.
- Then subtract your **floor penalty**. A score never goes below 0. Empty the floor line into the lid.
- After every round a pop-up shows the scores of every round so far, with a note for floor penalties and completed rows.

## The end

- The game ends after the wall-tiling of the first round in which somebody has a **complete horizontal row** of five.
- Bonuses: **+2** for each complete row, **+7** for each complete column, and **+10** for each colour whose five tiles are all on your wall. They appear as a final **Bonuses** row in the score table.
- The highest score wins. If tied, the player with the most complete rows wins. If still tied, they share the win.

## Free wall (optional)

- The host can switch on the **Free wall** variant in the lobby (off by default; everyone can see it).
- Walls start **blank**. When a line scores, you choose the column in that line's row for the tile, highlighted on your wall. A colour may not repeat in a row **or** in a column.
- A line may only be used while its row still has a free column where that colour fits. If earlier tiles in the same wall-tiling use up the last such place, the line's tiles go to the lid without scoring.
- If there is only one place for the tile it is placed for you. Everything else is as above, and the bonuses count in the same way.
