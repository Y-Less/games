# Scribblers

One player draws a secret word.  Everybody else types guesses.  The sooner you get it, the more you score.

## A turn

1. The drawer is offered a few words and picks one.  If they dither, one is picked for them.
2. They have the drawing time (90 seconds unless the host changed it) to draw it, with a pen, a rubber and a paint
   bucket in twenty-six colours.  No letters, no numbers: draw the thing.
3. Everybody else types guesses into the panel beside the board.  A guess that is nearly right ("hourse" for "house")
   is shown to the table and you alone are told you were close, so the others can learn from your near miss without
   being handed the answer.
4. As the time runs down, letters of the word are shown: one for a short word, up to three for a long one.
5. The turn ends when everybody has the word, or when the time runs out.  The word is shown, and the next person draws.

Everybody draws once a round, and the game is three rounds by default.  The scores go up at the end of each round.

## Scoring

- **A guesser** scores 100 for getting the word, plus up to 100 more for being quick (the bonus falls away over the
  drawing time), plus up to 60 for the letters that were still hidden when they guessed.  So a fast guess on a word
  with nothing revealed is worth about 260, and a guess in the last seconds of a long word about 100.
- **The drawer** scores the average of what the guessers scored.  A drawing everybody gets at once is worth as much to
  the drawer as to them; one nobody gets is worth nothing.
- Guessing costs nothing, so guess as often as you like.

## Words of your own

Anybody at the table can add words in the waiting room, and one of the words the drawer is offered each turn is one of
them while any are left unplayed.  They are kept for later games too, so a word added once keeps coming back.

## While you are drawing

The drawer can talk to the people who have already guessed, and they to each other, without the ones still guessing
seeing it.  Keys: **Q** pen, **W** paint bucket, **E** rubber, **1** to **4** brush size, **Ctrl+Z** undo.

## If somebody leaves

A person who leaves is replaced by a computer player, in their place and with their score, so nobody's turns are lost:
it draws and guesses like anybody else.  A drawer who has simply vanished mid-turn (their connection dropped) has the
turn given up instead: nobody scores and play moves on.
