# Cheat

A bluffing game for three to seven players. The first player to get rid of all their cards wins.

## Starting

- One player creates a game and shares the three-word code (or the invite link). The host fills the other seats with friends or **computer players**, chooses the number of players (3 to 7, normally 4) and starts the game once every seat is taken.
- The whole pack is dealt out, so some players may have one card more than others. With six or seven players two packs are used, so that there are enough cards to go round.
- The player holding the Ace of Spades goes first.

## Playing

- Each turn has a **rank to claim**. It begins with Aces, then 2s, 3s and so on up to Kings, and then starts again at Aces. The rank belongs to the turn, not the player.
- On your turn, put **one to four cards face down** on the pile and say they are all of the rank to claim. You **must** play at least one card, but the cards need not be what you say they are. Telling the truth is allowed; so is lying.
- Everyone sees how many cards you played, but not which.

## Calling "Cheat!"

- After a play, and before the next one, **any other player may call "Cheat!"**, saying they think the cards were not all of the claimed rank.
- The cards just played are turned over.
  - If any of them was **not** the claimed rank, the player who played them was cheating and **picks up the whole pile**.
  - If they **all** were the claimed rank, the caller was wrong and **picks up the whole pile**.
- Play then carries on with the next player and the next rank, whoever picked up the pile.
- Only the **last play** can be called. Once the next player has played, the earlier play stands.
- The computer players decide about each play quickly, but a person always has time to call first.

## Winning

- When you play your last card you have not won yet: the others may still call it. If someone calls and you were lying, you pick up the pile and the game goes on.
- If nobody calls it (everyone presses **I believe you**), or if somebody calls and you were telling the truth, **you win**.

## Tips

- Lying with one card is safer than lying with four, but telling the truth with several cards gets rid of them quicker.
- Keep a count of what you hold: if you have all four 7s and someone plays "three 7s", they must be lying (with one pack).
- A big pile is a big risk for a wrong call, but also a big punishment for a liar.
