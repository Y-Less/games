# Cribbage

A game for 2, 3, 4 or 5 players, everyone playing for themselves. The first to reach the target score wins: 121, or 61 for a short game. The host chooses the number of seats (empty seats can be filled with computer players) and the target.

## Dealing

The dealer owns the **crib**, an extra hand of four cards that is counted for the dealer at the end of the hand. The deal passes clockwise after every hand. Everyone ends up keeping four cards.

- **Two players:** six cards each. Each player puts two cards in the crib.
- **Three players:** five cards each, plus one extra card from the deck placed in the crib. Each player puts one card in the crib.
- **Four players:** five cards each. Each player puts one card in the crib.
- **Five players:** the dealer is dealt four cards and keeps them all; everyone else is dealt five and puts one card in the crib, which makes up the crib's four cards.

After everyone has discarded, the starter card is cut from the deck. If it is a Jack, the dealer pegs 2 for **his heels** at once.

## The play

Starting with the player to the dealer's left, players take turns to play one card face up, saying the running count (aces count 1, court cards 10). The count may never go over 31. A player who cannot play without going over 31 says **go**, which the game does for you; cards that would break 31 are disabled.

Points are pegged at once for:

- **Fifteen:** the count reaches 15, for 2.
- **Thirty-one:** the count reaches 31, for 2.
- **Pairs:** the card matches the one before it: a pair is 2, three of a kind is 6 and four of a kind is 12.
- **Runs:** the last three or more cards played form a run in any order (for example 4, 6, 5), one point per card. A card from outside the run breaks it.
- **Go or last card:** when nobody left can play, the last player to play scores 1 and the count starts again from 0 with the cards that remain, led by the next player in turn. The last card of the play scores 1 too, unless it made 31.

With three or more players, a player who cannot play is passed over while the others carry on; once nobody can play, the last player to have played scores the go.

## The show

When all the cards have been played, hands are counted with the starter, in order from the player to the dealer's left clockwise, with the dealer's hand last and then the dealer's crib. The game counts for you and lists every combination.

- **Fifteens:** every combination of cards adding up to 15 scores 2.
- **Pairs:** 2 for each pair of the same rank.
- **Runs:** 1 point per card for every run of three or more. Double and triple runs count each run separately, so a double run of three is 8 points with its pair.
- **Flush:** four cards of one suit in the hand scores 4, or 5 if the starter matches too. In the crib a flush counts only if the starter matches as well, for 5.
- **His nobs:** a Jack in the hand of the same suit as the starter scores 1.

There is no muggins: points are never stolen for missed scores.

## Winning

Points are pegged as they are earned, and the game ends the instant someone reaches the target, even in the middle of the play or the show. A winner who finishes while an opponent is still below 91 points (31 in the short game) has skunked them.

## Computer players and leaving

Computer players take a short moment to move. If you leave a game that is under way, a computer player takes your seat and the game carries on for the others (your result is not recorded). If nobody human is left, the game is abandoned.
