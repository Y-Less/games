## The idea

Dixnt is a card game for three or more friends.  Every card is a picture, and every turn starts with a word or phrase.  You play the card from your hand that fits the word best, then everyone talks about the cards and works out who was bluffing.

## Starting a game

One player starts a new game and becomes the host.  Everyone else joins with the three-word game code (such as `boss-aware-you`) or with the invite link.  The host chooses the play style, whether anybody chooses the word, the card categories, the hand size and the length of the game in the lobby, then starts the game once at least three players are there.

## How a turn works

A **turn** is one player's go as the chooser, and a **round** is everyone having had one.

1. The **chooser** (the role passes round the table in the order players joined) types a word or phrase.  The **Give me ideas** button suggests three.  With "No chooser" on (below) this step is skipped: a word is picked at random, and everyone plays a card.
2. Everybody else sees the word and plays one card from their hand that fits it.  The card leaves your hand as you play it.  In the two imposter styles, one random player sees ??? instead of the word and has to bluff.  You can swap your card for another until everyone has played.
3. All the cards and the real word are revealed.  Explain why your card fits, and listen to everyone else.
4. The turn is judged according to the play style (below).
5. Played cards are discarded, everyone draws back up to the hand size, and the next player becomes the chooser.  When the draw pile runs out, the discard pile is shuffled and used again.

## Play styles

The host picks one of three styles in the lobby.

### Everyone votes

- Every player votes for whoever they think was in the dark.  You cannot vote for yourself or for the chooser's card (the chooser plays none).  You can change your vote until the last one is in.
- The group is right only if the imposter has strictly the most votes.  A tie counts as wrong.
- If the group is right, each player who voted for the imposter scores 1 point.
- If the group is wrong, the imposter scores 1 point for every other player who did not vote for them.

### Only the chooser decides

- Only the chooser guesses who was in the dark, and that guess ends the turn at once.
- If the chooser is right, they score 1 point.
- If the chooser is fooled, the imposter scores 5 points.

### Favourite card

- Nobody is kept in the dark: everyone sees the word.
- The chooser picks the card they like best, which ends the turn.
- The player who played that card scores 2 points.

## No chooser

The host can also turn the chooser off altogether, with the tick box beside the play style.  Then:

- Nobody types a word.  One is picked at random for the whole table, and the turn begins with it already chosen.
- **Everybody** plays a card, the player whose turn it is included, so there is one more card on the table than usual.
- One random player still sees ??? instead of the word and still has to bluff.

It goes with the styles the whole table votes on, and with those alone.  "Only the chooser decides" and "Favourite card" are both one player deciding, and that player is the word-chooser, so neither can be played this way: the game cannot be set to both at once, whichever you pick first.

Nobody has a part of their own in such a turn, so nobody is marked out at the table: the word arrives, everybody plays, everybody votes.

## Length of the game

The host decides how many rounds the game runs for (1 to 10, two by default), which is how many times each player chooses the word — or, with no chooser, how many times each player's turn comes round.  Either way the game is the same length.  It ends once everyone has had all their turns, and the player with the most points wins.  If several players share the top score, they all win.  A skipped turn does not use up anyone's turn.

## Card categories

The cards come in categories: Fantasy, Fairy tales, Modern world, Sci-fi, Steampunk and Victorian, Medieval, Games and Food.  The host ticks which ones go into the deck.  There must be enough cards for every player's hand.

## Good to know

- If somebody leaves a game that is under way, a computer player takes their place and plays on with their cards and their score, so no turn is spoilt.  Somebody arriving in the middle — a friend slow to open the invitation, or the leaver coming back — takes a computer player's place the same way round, and gets their own place back if it is still being kept for them.  A game with nobody but people in it is full.
- You can be in one game at a time.
- The host can remove players, skip a turn (no points), or end the game, which returns everyone to the lobby with the scores reset.  A game ended early does not count towards your statistics.  Anybody the host removes, or the table votes out, cannot use the invitation to come back.
- If you lose your connection, just reopen the game: you carry on where you left off.
- A game closes when its last player leaves.  A finished game keeps its final scores for a day, so the invitation still shows them.
