## The idea

Draw! is a race.  **Two** players draw at the same time, each a word of their own, and everybody else sees both
drawings side by side with one box to type in.  The first drawing anybody names wins the turn.

## How a turn works

1. Both drawers are offered a few words and have **ten seconds** to pick one.  Dither, and the game picks for you:
   this is a quick-fire game.  The drawing starts as soon as both have picked.  The very first turn of a game is the
   exception: it has no clock, and waits until both drawers have picked.
2. Both of them draw at once.  A drawer sees their own board large, and the other drawing smaller beside it — they
   have no idea what it is either.
3. Everybody else races to type what either drawing is.  One box, two drawings: a guess is tried against both.
4. The **first** person to name either drawing ends the turn.  That drawer and that guesser each score a point, and
   nobody else scores anything.  If nobody names either drawing before the time runs out, nobody scores.
5. Both words go up for a moment, and the next pair draw.  There are no scores to close: the game simply carries on.

## Length of the game

A **round** is every pair of players having drawn against each other once, which at a table of four is six turns, and
everybody draws three times.  The host can set the game to run for more than one round.  Nobody ever draws three
turns running.

## Good to know

- The two drawers do not type.  One of them naming the other's drawing would be a race nobody else could win, and
  naming their own would give it away.
- You are never shown a letter of either word, only how long it is, over the board it belongs to — a turn is over too quickly for clues.
- If somebody leaves a game that is under way, a computer player takes their place and carries on with the drawing
  they had already started.  Somebody arriving takes a computer player's place the same way round.
- The player with the most points wins; if several share the top score, they all win.
