# Trivium

A quiz that never stops to ask whether you are ready.  Two to eight players, and the whole thing runs on the clock:
there is nothing to press between questions and nothing to close between rounds.

## A round

Each round begins with the wheel.  Its wedges are the ten categories, and each one is as wide as that category has
questions to ask, so Film & TV is a fat slice and a thinly stocked category is a thin one — though never so thin that
the pointer cannot find it.  Where it stops is where the round's questions come from.

Then the questions come, one at a time, with the answers on big buttons.  Press the one you think is right.  **Your
first press is your answer**: there is no changing your mind, because how quickly you answered is half of what it is
worth.  The question closes when everybody has answered, or after fifteen seconds, whichever comes first — and a
computer player that has already decided to sit this one out is not somebody the table waits for.

## The reveal

Nobody sees anybody else's answer until the question has closed.  Then every answer is shown in the order it arrived:
one marker at a time travels from its player's name up to the exact spot on the exact button that player pressed.  A
player who did not answer keeps their marker where it is.  Once every marker has landed, the right answer lights up
green, and a moment later the next question goes up.

## Scoring

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Any right answer | 5 points |
| and the quickest right answer, a further | 5 points |
| the next quickest | 4, then 3, then 2, then 1 |
| the sixth right answer onwards | nothing extra |
| a wrong answer, or no answer | nothing |

So the most one question can be worth is 10, and a slow right answer is still worth far more than a quick wrong one.
Highest total when the last question has been asked wins; equal totals share the win.

## Settings

The host chooses how many rounds (1 to 8) and how many questions each round holds (1 to 10), and anybody can pick
their own marker colour in the waiting room.

## Computer players

The host can fill the room with computer players.  They vary: one knows a lot but thinks it over, one is in almost
before the question has finished appearing, one is guessing, and one leaves it so late that it sometimes misses the
question altogether.

## Where the questions come from

From [The Trivia API](https://the-trivia-api.com) and from the site's own question bank, mixed in proportion to how
many each of them holds.  The whole site draws on one stock of them, so nobody is asked the same question twice in a
sitting, here or anywhere else on the site.
